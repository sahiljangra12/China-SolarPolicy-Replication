Q&A

Questions answered

Do the paper's results reproduce?

Yes. Running the authors' code with only file paths changed reproduces all 12 numbers checked, to four decimals. See the check.

Why does the main estimate shrink in a fair comparison?

PSI is missing for 724 counties and set to 0. They are 694 of 847 urban districts but only 9 of 1,497 rural counties, so the regression mostly compares rural counties with urban districts. Birdwatching grew much faster in cities. Comparing urban with urban and rural with rural gives −0.0027 (p = 0.47). Details.

What does “83% of urban county-months have imputed PSI” mean?

Of all urban county-months in the regression, 83.1% belong to counties whose PSI was missing and filled in as 0. For rural the share is 1.2%. The real-PSI sample drops 20,364 rows, 98.7% of them urban. log

Where does the paper analyze the number of birdwatchers?

Only inside the control Duration Table S1, SM p. 24. In the authors' file the variable is a count of submitted records per county-month, so it measures checklists, not people. No table uses birdwatching as an outcome.

Is there evidence that birding grew faster in cities?

Yes. The share of months with any record grew 2.3 percentage points a year faster in urban districts, and the log number of records 0.060 a year faster. log

How little confounding would overturn the result?

A confounder explaining 2.16% of leftover variation in both PSI and Shannon brings the estimate to zero; 1.24% makes it insignificant. Try it.

Why did the first sensitivity run fail, and how was it fixed?

Explicit county dummies need 2,344 variables; Stata/BE allows 2,048. Residualizing every variable on the fixed effects first gives exactly the same coefficient and lets sensemakr run. log

Is the instrument weak?

Its F is 30.2 with county clusters, 10.8 with city clusters, 2.8 with province clusters. But Anderson–Rubin sets, valid with weak instruments, still exclude zero. The problem is exclusion, not strength.

Is the plausibly-exogenous IV step valid?

As a sensitivity check, yes. As a corrected estimate, no: the direct effect is measured in mostly urban counties. It shows the IV conclusion depends on an assumption the data contradict. Try other values.

Where does the solar-area data come from? Is it missing for the same counties as PSI?

Licensed plants from the National Energy Administration Table S2, SM p. 26. It is missing for 1,184 counties; only 473 of them also lack PSI. The file has 11,699 true zeros, so missing counties can be told apart from counties with no plants.

Which staggered estimators were run?

Callaway & Sant'Anna, Sun & Abraham, Borusyak, Jaravel & Spiess, and both de Chaisemartin & D'Haultfœuille packages. Compare the event studies.

Why do dCDH and the binary estimators disagree?

They answer different questions: the effect of more policy versus the effect of a first policy. Most PSI variation is later increases in counties already treated in 2014, which only dCDH uses.

Was the “no stayers” dCDH method used?

No. It needs all units untreated at the start and one common adoption date. Here 838 counties already have PSI above 0 in 2014, timing varies, and about 74% of year-to-year steps are stayers. So the stayers-based estimator was used.

What could not be run?

did_multiplegt_dyn with controls() and with continuous(), and explicit-dummy sensitivity, all because of Stata/BE limits. Workarounds are documented; all three can run in Stata/MP or R.

Are the live tools real estimates?

Yes. Tools A, C and D read Stata results computed in advance. Tool B uses closed-form formulas with Stata inputs and reproduces sensemakr to four decimals. code

Why is the paper itself not posted here?

It is under copyright. Load your own copy on the Paper check page and every reference opens at the exact page, inside this site.

Glossary

Shannon index
A diversity score: higher when more species are recorded and records are spread evenly.
PSI
Policy Stringency Index: how many, and how binding, solar-promotion documents a county has in a year.
TWFE / fixed effects
A regression that compares each county with itself over time, net of shocks common to all counties in a month.
Imputed PSI
PSI that was missing and filled in as 0 by the authors.
Confounder
Something that affects both the policy and the outcome, creating a non-causal link.
Robustness value
How strong a hidden confounder must be, as a share of leftover variation, to bring the estimate to zero.
Instrumental variable
A variable that shifts the treatment and should affect the outcome only through it (the exclusion restriction).
Anderson–Rubin set
A confidence interval for an IV estimate that stays valid when the instrument is weak.
Staggered DiD
Difference-in-differences when units start treatment at different times.
Pre-trend (placebo) test
Checks whether treated and comparison units were already diverging before treatment.
Stayers and switchers
Units whose treatment stays the same, and units whose treatment changes, between two periods.
Clustering
Allowing errors to be correlated within a group (county, city, province) when computing standard errors.
n.s. and stars
n.s. = not significant at 5%. ** p < 0.05, *** p < 0.01.
